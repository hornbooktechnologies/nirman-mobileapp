# Homepage storyboard

Status: proposed composition and draft copy. The narrative follows the user's suggested flow; exact layouts and sequencing will be refined through visual review.

## Narrative arc

Recognise real site work → recognise your working arrangement → see daily activity organised → understand the records → feel comfortable using the product → request a demo.

The page must remain complete and understandable when media is paused and motion is reduced.

## 1. Cinematic hero

- Message: “Built for the way construction actually works.”
- Composition: large editorial headline, concise supporting copy, warm site photograph fading into cream, prominent phone.
- Product: short real dashboard/workforce/expense overview; avoid cramming every module into the opening.
- Actions: Request Demo and Watch Overview.
- Motion: controlled phone entrance, short app sequence, one or two supporting details.
- Required assets: hero photograph, branded logo, authentic app recording or screenshots, poster.

## 2. Important information is scattered

- Message: “Important site records shouldn't be hard to find.”
- Composition: editorial text with restrained fragments representing a register, message, photo, and expense note.
- Motion: fragments align into one organised project view.
- Guardrail: avoid implying that ordinary communication disappears or integrations import all of these sources automatically.

## 3. Different people run construction differently

- Message: “You don't all run construction the same way.”
- Composition: distinct working arrangements shown as concise scenes or selectable stories.
- Cover: several projects, one hands-on builder, delegated execution, overlapping responsibilities, independent contractor.
- Guardrail: no mandatory three-person hierarchy or worker login implication.

## 4. NirmanSite fits those arrangements

- Message: “Keep your way of working. Bring the records together.”
- Composition: a shared product stage changes context around the chosen arrangement.
- Product: verified project/context views and responsibilities, without displaying technical permission jargon.
- Motion: context transitions, anchored to a stable phone or project stage.
- Relationship: sections 3 and 4 may become one two-part signature sequence if this improves pacing.

## 5. A short day at the site

- Message: show a few recognisable moments from daily work.
- Composition: immersive product stage, optionally sticky on desktop; sequential on mobile.
- Product: dashboard → workforce → recorded expense → summary, with a clear outcome at each step.
- Guardrail: this is a short cinematic overview, not a training tutorial or a long scroll trap.

## 6. Workforce, attendance, wages and Kharchi

- Message: “From today's work to the next wage settlement.”
- Composition: large phone with an enlarged worker or wage detail, paired with short practical copy.
- Demonstration: attendance record, already-paid Kharchi recorded, then its place in wage settlement.
- Motion: a focused 5–10 second clip or two separately controlled short examples if readability requires it.
- Guardrail: preserve actual app workflow; never show the worker initiating an app request or an in-app money transfer without support.

## 7. Understand recorded spending

- Message: “Know where your site money is going.”
- Composition: potential green contrast section; real expense screen and enlarged summaries.
- Demonstration: an expense is recorded, followed by supported project/category/period summaries.
- Guardrail: chart types and values must match supported product output. No inferred profit, savings, accounting, or bank balance.

## 8. Materials and progress

- Message: “Follow what arrives. See what moves forward.”
- Composition: construction photograph or material detail paired with status and progress screens, avoiding another uniform card grid.
- Demonstration: verified request/status/delivery activity and a separate progress update.
- Guardrail: do not imply a material delivery automatically creates an expense or progress event unless verified.

## 9. A familiar language

- Message: “Built for your work. In a language you're comfortable with.”
- Composition: the same meaningful app view in English, Hindi, and Gujarati, with controlled switching or carefully arranged previews.
- Motion: short crossfade; allow manual selection and pause for reading.
- Guardrail: use real translated screens, consistent demo data, and script-appropriate sizing.

## 10. Supporting sales activity

- Message: “Keep enquiries moving alongside site work.”
- Composition: compact supporting section, visually quieter than workforce or financial clarity.
- Product: verified enquiry, follow-up, site visit, unit or booking detail.
- Guardrail: do not make sales the primary product category.

## 11. Shaped by real conversations

- Message: different working practices shaped the product.
- Composition: quiet editorial statement; optional authentic founder photograph or site image when available.
- Guardrail: no fabricated interview quote, research count, or testimonial.

## 12. Final invitation

- Message: “See how NirmanSite fits your site.”
- Composition: generous space, strong typography, one calm product or construction detail.
- Action: Request Demo; optional overview link if useful.
- Footer: verified contact, navigation, and legal destinations once supplied.

## Consistency without repetition

Use the same palette, type system, photo treatment, phone framing, and motion curves throughout. Vary scale, alignment, media treatment, and density. Only the main demonstration should move at a time; quiet sections create breathing room.
